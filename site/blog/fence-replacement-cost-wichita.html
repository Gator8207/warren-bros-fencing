How Much Does It Cost to Replace a Fence in Wichita?

Replacement = tear-out plus a new fence. Here's how the two pieces add up, and when replacing beats repairing.

Home › Guides › Fence Replacement Cost

A replacement is really two jobs in one: remove the old fence, then install the new one. So the cost is the price of the new fence plus a removal line for hauling out what's already there.

For a standard 6-foot cedar privacy fence, the new install runs $40 to $55 per linear foot. Add removal of the old fence, and most 100-foot cedar replacements land in roughly the $4,500–$6,500 range.

Replace or repair? When replacement is the better value

Replacement makes sense when you're past the point of patching:

  • The run is widely rotted or leaning in multiple spots — fix one section and the next one fails
  • The fence is at or past its 15–20 year service life
  • You're changing height, style, or material (e.g. old chain link to cedar privacy)
  • Repeated repair bills are adding up to more than one clean replacement

If the damage is localized and the rest of the fence is sound, repair is the cheaper, smarter move — see what fence repair costs in Wichita. We'll tell you honestly which camp you're in instead of selling you the bigger job.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace a fence in Wichita?

A replacement is the cost of the new fence plus removal of the old one. A new 6-foot cedar privacy fence runs $40-$55 per linear foot installed, and removing/hauling the old fence adds a separate line item. Most 100-foot cedar replacements land in roughly the $4,500-$6,500 range once removal is included. The exact number depends on what's coming out and what's going up.

Does fence replacement include removing the old fence?

Yes — a true replacement includes tearing out and hauling away the old fence before the new one goes up. We price removal as its own line item so you can see exactly what it adds. A wood fence is straightforward; chain link or posts set in concrete take more labor to pull.

Should I repair or replace my fence?

Repair if the damage is localized and the rest of the fence is sound — it's a fraction of replacement cost. Replace if the run is widely rotted, leaning in multiple spots, or past its 15-20 year service life, where repeated repairs would cost more than one clean replacement. We give you the honest call at the free estimate.

How long does a fence replacement take?

Most residential replacements take 2-3 days including tear-out. Larger jobs — over 200 linear feet, multiple gates, slope work, or stubborn concrete-set posts to remove — can run 3-5 days. We confirm the timeline at the estimate.
